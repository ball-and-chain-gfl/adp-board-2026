# 2026 ADP Board — Sleeper vs Winks / Yahoo / ESPN

Draft-day board for one specific league: 12-team **Sleeper**, **full PPR**,
1QB / 2RB / 2WR / 1TE / 1FLEX / 1K / 1DEF.

It puts Sleeper's ADP for every draftable player next to three other sources, so you can see
where your Sleeper room is mispricing someone — and it doubles as a live board you click players
off of as they go.

**Live:** https://adp-board-2026.vercel.app

> Originally built against ESPN. Swapped to Sleeper on 2026-09-07: Sleeper is now the spine and
> ESPN is a comparison column. See **Sleeper is the spine** below for what that changed.

## Trade Analyzer tab

`/trade.html`, linked from the tab to the right of "Sleeper ADP". It is a **hand-written page,
not generated by `build.py`** — edit `trade.html` directly. The tab link itself lives in the
`build.py` template (and in the committed `index.html`).

It analyses one ESPN team — The Bryan Football Team, team 10 in league 1327340807 — and is
**read-only**: nothing is ever written to ESPN.

Five sections, built around one question: where can I reasonably improve my team? On a wide
screen they are tabs under the site bar (there is no team header); on a phone they are a bar
along the bottom.

**Player card.** Tapping a player's name or photo anywhere opens a card: what he does for my
lineup (the two period tags), projected totals for the rest of the season and the playoffs, his
schedule week by week with the projection for each game and the opponent shaded by matchup, a
plain-language line on how his number is built, his games so far, the stats for his position,
my status and lineup calls, and a button to the packages that would get him (or to shop him).
The rest of a row still does what it did: on Targets it opens his packages; in the builder it
ticks the player into the deal (each row has a checkbox), while his name or photo opens the
card.

Photos are rounded tiles on a tint of the position's colour; top-target cards alone show the
photo at full height on the left, fading into the card. Lineup gains are coloured pills, so a list reads by colour before it
reads by number. Both pages use Inter (Google Fonts) with tabular figures. The
page uses a light theme in the style of a fantasy-sports site (white panels on light grey, a dark
top bar, one blue for actions, green = good for me, red = bad for me, amber = maybe), and shows
each fantasy team's own ESPN logo (`logo` from `mTeam`) next to its name.

- **Targets** — what opens first:

    see only the players who fix it.

    weeks 1.5×) that some team would actually deal — at least one package with a *Likely yes*
    or *Worth asking* answer.
  - *Everyone who helps*: a sortable table of every healthy player on another roster who would
    make my lineup better (a quarter-point a week or more), with the best answer any package for
    him gets. *Gettable* narrows it to the realistic ones. Also *Free agents* and *Sell-highs*.
  Tapping any target opens Packages filtered to the deals that would get him, every answer shown.
  The table's *Replaces* column and each top-target card name the starter of mine he would take
  the place of.
- **Roster** — the rank tiles at the top each have a *see all* link: the whole league at that
  spot (every team's starters there and their total) or by lineup, with my row marked. On the
  Targets tab the same link sits on the need tiles.
  **Everyone is read against my lineup**, with two tags a player: one for the rest of the
  regular season, one for the playoff weeks. The playoff tag shows each playoff game on its
  own first — his rank at the position that week, coloured by tier, or BYE/OUT — with the
  three-week average underneath.
  - *My players*: how good he will be — his rank at the position over those weeks. *Elite* is
    the top third of the startable ranks, then *Starter*, *Fringe* (the next half again) and
    *Bench*; *Out* when he misses more than half of them.
  - *Everyone else*: what he does to my lineup. **▲ +4.1** is the points a week my lineup gains
    with him in it, byes and injuries counted, and the line under it names the starter he'd go
    "over" (or "sharing weeks with", when he doesn't out-project him but the pair scores more
    by matchup and bye). **▼ −2.3** is how far he sits behind the weakest starter he could
    replace. Within half a point is *Even*.
  The All / Upgrades / Playoff upgrades / Downgrades buttons filter on those tags. There is no
  "avoid" tag: touchdown luck and hot efficiency are notes in the *Read* column, not a verdict.
  Below that, need tiles and the players — **one table per position** (QB, RB, WR, TE, K/DEF),
  each with only that position's stats: my roster, any team, every rostered

Every list is a table. **On a phone nothing scrolls sideways and nothing scrolls inside
something else**: each row keeps the player and the two or three numbers that matter (grade
and gains on Targets, bid and term on Waivers, the two period tags on the Roster), and the

mean something for the position are shown: QB = xFP, +/−, trend, upside, rush share, first
downs; RB adds snaps, targets, opportunities, red-zone work, TDs and rushing efficiency; WR/TE

column is blank for positions it doesn't apply to (`REL` in `trade.html`).
- **Packages** — filters on the left (on a phone, behind a *Filters & my players* button), one

- **Builder** — my players, the deal, their players, in 2-team or 3-team mode. The deal panel
  explains how every roster fits after the trade. On a phone the deal is a bar pinned under the
  list; tap it for details.
- **Waivers** — every free agent worth a bid, with the bid, what he adds to my lineup and who to
  drop for him; *Show every free agent* lists the rest.

### My lineup calls (starter / bench / handcuff)

The **Lineup** menu beside each of my players (Roster tab, my roster) overrides the automatic
lineup: *Always start*, *Bench: never start*, or *Handcuff: starts if X out*. A bench call never
fills a starting spot in the lineup maths; with the waiver floor on (trade valuation) that means
losing him costs my lineup nothing, which is the point: a handcuff is trade fodder, not a flex
play. A handcuff moves up to a regular in any week his starter is out (injured, not on bye), so
my injury calls feed straight into it. Bench calls don't count toward my startable depth, and the
waiver drop suggestion never names a starter call or a handcuff. Calls are kept in the browser
(`localStorage`, key `trade.role`); `ROLE_SEED` in `trade.html` is the default on every device
until changed (Kyle Monangai = handcuff to D'Andre Swift).

### The weeks I play the other team, and byes

`api/trade.js` sends my remaining regular-season opponents (`opps`, from `mMatchupScore`). For
every deal, the week I play a team in it is scored on its own: what the trade does to THEIR
lineup that week. That swing is added to my gain when packages are ranked ("Best for me"), and
called out on the card and in the builder — e.g. trading Trey McBride to a team I meet in his
bye week helps them every week but the one that matters to me. Incoming players with a bye
still ahead are flagged too. The *My lineup* filter hides any deal that doesn't leave me better
off once that swing is counted; "Any, even if it costs me" shows them.

### Three-team trades

Two shapes, both judged with the same rules as a 2-team deal, each team on its own roster,
the deal's answer being the worst of theirs. Only three-ways where every team can say yes are
listed, and one is dropped when the straight deal with B (same players out, same back) is
already as good.

- **Rotation.** Everything of mine goes to team C, C sends one player to team B, B sends one
  or two to me.
- **Hub.** I send one player to B and one to C; C still sends one to B. This is the shape for
  "my back for a better back": B only gives up its better back if a back comes back (mine)
  *and* something makes up the difference (C's player), and C is paid with my other player. A
  rotation can't do that, because B would be left short at the position. On 11/10 the hub
  took three-team deals from 3 to 25.

The search skips a candidate only on exact bounds — value can only shrink once it lands
(`USE` ≤ 1), so if what a team gets is under 88% of what it gives at full value it can't reach
*Worth asking* — plus the same hole and top-piece tests, so nothing that could pass is skipped
(`threeWays` in `trade.html`).

In the builder's 3-team mode, tapping one of my players sends him to C, a second tap sends him
to B, a third takes him out; each picked row shows where he's going.

**When nothing works.** Picking a target shows *what his team would need*: about 88% of what
he is worth to them, led by one piece worth at least 60% of him, plus a starter back at his
position if losing him leaves them short — and which of my players are big enough to lead.
That is usually the answer to "surely a third team can make this work": a third team can add
the missing piece, but it has to be paid with something of mine that starts for it.

### Waivers and FAAB

**How long he helps.** Every remaining week is scored on its own, the points he adds to that
week's lineup. A week counts at +1 or more. *Long-term*: useful in at least 60% of the weeks
left, and valued at his full gain. *Short-term*: two or more weeks, valued at his gain times the
share of that 60% he covers. *1-week fill-in*: one week only — worth a dollar or two if that
week is this week, and nothing today otherwise, because he can be picked up the week he's
needed. Rivals are priced the same way, so "Rivals" counts the teams he'd be a long-term starter
for.

Every free agent in the pool is priced by what he adds to each team's lineup from now to week
17, byes and injuries counted, playoff weeks at 1.5×. What a team should pay is a share of the
FAAB it has left:

    share = min(70%, ((gain − 0.5) / 10) ^ 1.5)        gain = lineup points a week he adds

The first half-point is free (a body to cover one bye can be had that week for $0–5), so +1 a
week ≈ 1% of a budget, +2 ≈ 6%, +3 ≈ 12%, +5 ≈ 30%, +8 or more 65–70%. Nobody adding under half
a point a week gets a bid. The **bid** is what beats
the rival who wants him most (their share of *their* remaining budget, plus $1), capped at what
he is worth to me; when that rival can outbid my ceiling the card says so. Budgets come from
ESPN (`acquisitionBudgetSpent` per team). The drop suggestion is the player whose loss costs my
lineup least once the pickup is in. Kickers and defences aren't in the free-agent pool.

### Expert notes (Norris, Winks)

`api/trade.js` reads the two analysts' Yahoo author pages on every refresh (so at most 30 min
old). There is no feed: each listing's `data-ylk` attribute carries the title (`slk`) and link
(`tar_uri`), and player names are matched against the link's **slug**, because titles are cut
at the first apostrophe and slugs never are. The weekly waiver show is a podcast page; its
`othertags` list names every player covered. Clips cut from that show only say "waiver wire
option" in their description, so up to 12 clips that name a free agent are fetched to check.
The articles are not shown on the page; they are used. A free agent named in a waiver piece is
one the analysts think has a new role the season numbers haven't caught up with, so he is
projected on his **last three games alone** instead of the season blend (only ever upward), and
that flows into his lineup value and FAAB bid like anyone else's. He carries an *EXPERT* tag. If
Yahoo is unreachable or changes its markup, nobody gets the adjustment and everything else works.

### Where the projections come from

**ESPN.** Every remaining week uses ESPN's own projected points for that player, read from
ESPN's public full-PPR default league (`leaguedefaults/3`, `kona_player_info`, one call per
week, the 500 most-owned players). That league's scoring is the same as this one's, kickers
and defenses included, and it needs no login, so free agents are covered too. The payload
says so in `proj` (`src`, players covered, weeks loaded): 392 of 415 players on 11/10.

- A week ESPN projects at **zero** (out, or not expected to play) counts as out. It is flagged
  `z` and still carries the player's average for the weeks he does play, so overruling it with
  *Healthy* under "My call" gives a real number.
- `base` is ESPN's average over the weeks he plays; `own` keeps this tool's number for
  reference.
- ESPN's projections already carry injuries, so the status-and-return-date logic only applies
  to the few players ESPN doesn't cover.

**Fallback.** Anyone ESPN has no projection for (a few deep free agents) keeps this tool's own
model: half workload (xFP, a model refitted on this season's player-games that prices carries,
red-zone carries, targets and red-zone targets by position), half scoring, times a matchup
factor capped at ±20%. If ESPN's feed is down, everyone falls back to it. The workload model
is still what the *Read* column, the stats and the Role signal are built on.

### How a player is read (Targets)

Everything is in points a game against what he has been scoring:

- **owed** — xFP minus PPG: his workload says more than the box score.
- **touchdown luck** — expected touchdowns from his carries and targets (`xTD`, a second model
  on the same four inputs) minus touchdowns scored. This is the part that evens out, so it
  carries the most weight (0.75). League-wide the model is honest: 589 expected vs 574 scored
  when it was built.
- **efficiency** — the rest of the gap. Part luck, part the player, so it counts less (0.35).
- **role** — a role is a share of the offence, not a box score, and it differs by position:
  - *QB*: binary. He is the starter or he isn't, and only games he plays are counted, so a
    starting quarterback's role never "shrinks" after bad games. No role signal at all.
  - *RB*: his share of the team's carries and of its targets, each priced by what the xFP
    model says a carry and a target are worth to a back, on a league-average team workload.
  - *WR, TE*: the same, where target share does nearly all the work.
  A role **change** needs two witnesses: that share moved *and* his playing time (snap share)
  moved the same way, last three games against all the ones before. The change is the smaller
  of the two. A receiver who ran every route but drew fewer targets for three weeks has not
  lost his role. It counts 0.6 × the change × his xFP, and is flagged at ±15%.
- **schedule** — average rank of the defences ahead against the ones already faced.

"Upside" is the sum. The buy grade is half what he adds to my lineup, a quarter upside, and the
rest his workload percentile and how soft his playoff weeks are.

**Hurt players are never targets.** Anyone out, doubtful, on IR or suspended is left off the
Starters list and out of every suggested package until I overrule ESPN's status myself.

### How a trade is judged

Not two price tags — two rosters.

- **Points over replacement.** A player's value is what he scores above the best free agent
  you could start at his position instead. The Targets tab states it (replacement level at each
  position, who is scarcer and by how much, and the depth behind it) and the Targets table and
  the player card carry each player's *Over repl.* number.
- **Is there a running-back premium?** Yes. The 24th back has scored 11.2 a game this season
  and 33 receivers are at or above that (the 36th receiver scores 11.0): the league holds
  about 36 startable receivers for every 24 startable backs. Checked on 11/10 rather than assumed. On ESPN's weekly
  projections the best free-agent backs after the first project to **6.7** a game against
  **8.9** for receivers, so a back is about 2 points a game harder to replace, and the 36th
  back has scored 8.9 a game this season against 11.0 for the 36th receiver. Starter-level
  depth is even (12 backs and 10 receivers project 16+; the 24th of each sits ~4 over its own
  replacement). Published multi-season work puts a top back at roughly 1.3× a top receiver's
  value over replacement. The premium needs no special rule: value is measured against each
  position's own waiver wire (next bullet), so it is whatever that gap is today, and the
  Targets tab prints the current replacement levels and who is scarcer.
  Sources: [Fantasy Points, WAR position trends](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2024/fantasy-war-position-trends),
  [The Fantasy Footballers, positional replacement value](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/positional-replacement-value-by-draft-round-in-fantasy-football/),
  [Sharp Football Analysis, replacement value](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/fantasy-football-high-scorers-replacement-value-and-repeating-starting-weeks/amp/).
- **Position scarcity.** Trade value is points over the waiver wire *at the same position*
  (average of the 2nd–4th best free agents). Where free agents are thin a starter is worth more
  than an equal scorer at a position you can replace for free. The Targets header shows the
  league's last-starter line and the free-agent line for each position.
- **Waiver floor.** Lineups are scored with every starting spot worth at least the free-agent
  line, so a deal is measured against the free alternative rather than an empty slot.
- **Startable** is a rank at the position across every player, rostered or not: QB12, RB24,
  WR24, TE12, K12, DEF12. Depth is how many of *those* a team holds — short, set or deep — not
  how many bodies it has at the position.
- **Holes.** If a deal leaves either team with fewer startable players at a position than it
  has spots, and it had them before, that side says no. A team with two backs does not give one
  up unless one comes back.
- **Worth to them.** A player counts in full for the team getting him only if he would start;
  70% as their flex, 15% on their bench at RB/WR, 5% at a one-starter spot (QB, TE).
- **Something has to play for them.** If nothing they get would start or flex, it's a no. A
  backup QB or TE they'd never play is a wasted roster spot: any deal that hands them one is a
  no.
- **No sideways moves.** When everything in and out is at one position, it needs a clear
  upgrade for them: +1.5 a week to be *Worth asking*, +2.5 to be *Likely* (Purdy for Love is a
  shrug). Any other deal needs +0.35 a week to ask, +0.75 to be likely. Their own starters cost a premium to pry loose and
  their bench is cheap. Value also climbs faster than points: one player 12 over the waiver
  wire is worth more than two who are 6 over.
- **A starter moves for a starter.** If they give up someone in their lineup, someone coming
  back has to go into it. And the best player in a deal does not move for a pile of lesser ones.
- **Their lineup** has to come out ahead as well.

Packages are tagged *Likely yes*, *Worth asking* or *Long shot*; nothing that helps my lineup is
hidden, except offers under 45% of the asked-for value, which are skipped as absurd. The builder
shows all of it for any deal, including the ones that fail, and previews the lineup after the
trade slot by slot — who comes in for whom, the points change at each position and the league
rank before and after — for either team.

### Injuries, and ruling players out by hand

ESPN's fantasy status says *Out* whether a player misses a week or the season, so
`api/trade.js` also reads ESPN's injury feed, which publishes an expected return date, and
turns it into the week he is due back (or "not this season"). That feed lists only 25 players a
team. For anyone it misses: on IR or suspended and not playing, or out two games running, is
treated as gone until told otherwise.

The **Status** menu beside every player shows that reading — "Out for season", "On IR, back wk
12", "On IR, no return date" — until you pick something else: Healthy, out for the next week,
out through any later week, or out for the season. Your calls are saved in the browser
(`localStorage`, key `trade.ov`), drop off by themselves once the week has passed, and feed
everything.

### Data

- `api/trade.js` builds the whole payload and edge-caches it for 30 minutes. It also carries
  each team's FAAB left (`faab`) and the expert notes (`experts`).
- League rosters, records and lineup slots come through the GFL dashboard's ESPN proxy
  (`gfl-dashboard.vercel.app/api/espn`). The league is private and that proxy already holds the
  login, so this project carries no ESPN credentials. If that proxy is down, the tab is down.
- Everything else comes from Sleeper's weekly stat feed, for every rostered player plus the
  best 200 unrostered ones (30 QB, 60 RB, 80 WR, 30 TE):
  - share of the offence: snap share, target share, carry share, red-zone share, air-yard
    share, WOPR, opportunities and red-zone opportunities per game
  - receiving: targets per snap, catch rate, yards per target, catch depth, YAC per catch
  - rushing: yards per carry, yards after contact per carry, broken tackles per carry
  - touchdowns scored and expected, first downs per game, the last three games against the
    season, and how tough the schedule faced so far has been
- **xFP** and **xTD** are refitted on every load from this season's own player-games: carries,
  red-zone carries, targets, red-zone targets — separately for RB, WR and TE.
- Defence-vs-position is built from the same feed. Ranks follow ESPN's convention: 1 = stingiest,
  32 = softest. Opponent adjustment is half the gap to league average, capped at ±20%.
- Each remaining week through 17 gets a projection: half usage (xFP), half production, on the
  **season**, times the opponent adjustment, zero on a bye. The last three games only get a say
  when the role itself changed (above): up to 60% of the weight once it has moved by a quarter
  or more. A cold or hot stretch in the same role changes nothing.

### Known limits

- **Sleeper's `rec_air_yd` is air yards on catches only** (`rec_yd = rec_air_yd + rec_yar`),
  not air yards on every target. So there is no true aDOT: "Depth" is air yards per catch, and
  air-yard share and WOPR are built on completed air yards. For the same reason air yards are
  kept out of xFP — they are an outcome, not an opportunity.
- No routes run in any free feed, so no yards or targets per route; targets per snap stands in.
- No PFF grades, and no projections but its own.
- The willingness rules are a model of how managers behave, not a survey of this league.
- **Scoring is full PPR**, checked against the league's ESPN settings on every load (reception
  = 1; the payload carries `scoring`). Points come from Sleeper's `pts_ppr`, which matches
  ESPN's settings here except interceptions: ESPN charges −2, Sleeper assumes −1, so each
  interception is docked the difference.
- A played week that comes back empty from Sleeper is treated as a failed fetch: one retry,
  then the API fails (502, not cached) rather than build projections on part of the season.

Sleeper and ESPN spell a few team codes differently (`WAS`/`WSH`); `FIX` in `api/trade.js`
maps them. Players are joined ESPN-to-Sleeper on normalised name + position.

## Build

```bash
python3 build.py
```

Stdlib Python only — no dependencies, no install step. `build.py` holds the entire
HTML/CSS/JS template as a Python string and writes `index.html` next to itself.
`index.html` is generated but committed, because Vercel serves it as a static file and does
not run Python.

**Edit the template inside `build.py`.** Direct edits to `index.html` are destroyed on the
next build.

## What it shows

One view, keyed to **Sleeper ADP**. Sleeper publishes a single metric — its "rank" is just that
ADP re-numbered — so there is nothing to put on a second tab. The old Rank vs Rank / ADP vs ADP
strip is gone.

Column order is fixed: `Avg of 3 | # Player | Sleeper ADP | Winks rk | Yahoo | ESPN`.

Three of the four numbers are ADP and directly comparable. **Winks publishes rankings only**, so
his column is labelled `rk` and rendered as an integer — same scale, different thing.

The board runs 18 rounds / 216 picks. Depth is `TEAMS_N * 18` in `build.py`, and Sleeper's live
ADP defines who is on it.

### Colour

The three comparison columns are shaded by their gap from the Sleeper baseline, with the signed
gap printed small beside each number:

- **green** — the player goes *earlier* on that source than on Sleeper; it likes him more than
  your room does
- **red** — he goes *later*; Sleeper is paying up
- gaps under 3 picks are a flat dead zone, full saturation at ±25

The **Sleeper cell** carries the consensus hue — how many of the three other sources take him at
least 3 picks earlier than Sleeper:

| Sources agreeing | Colour |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | none — one source disagreeing isn't a signal |
| 2 | blue |
| 3 | purple |

Hue says how many agree; brightness says by how much.

### Once the draft starts, every number column goes live

Before the first pick, all four number columns show the static read above. Once players start
coming off the board they switch to a **draft clock** measured against the pick actually on the
clock (players taken, plus one):

- **red** — not due yet
- **yellow** — the consensus says he's due, but Sleeper's own board won't reach him for a while,
  so you can wait
- **green** — he's slid past his market price and he's there for you

The handover is `TAKEN` going above zero. A clock reading before pick 1 says nothing, since every
number is ahead of it — so the pre-draft state is the gap read, and the whole market is legible
while you prep. The yellow override measures against **Sleeper's own number**, because Sleeper is
the room you're drafting in.

The `rd n` beside the average is the round the consensus average lands in.

Players are grouped into positional **tiers**, cut where the board has a real gap. The orange
`cliff` tag marks the last player before a drop.

### Interaction

- **click a row** — player off the board, pick advances
- **Undo**, or Ctrl/Cmd+Z — restores the most recent
- **restore all** — clears the board, pick back to 1
- position filter is single-select
- below 1024px the board narrows to three columns — average, player, Winks. **The Sleeper column
  is the one hidden**, not Winks: every remaining cell prints its gap to Sleeper, and the `#` and
  round slot already carry Sleeper's own position, since the board is ordered by it. The consensus
  hue moves off the Sleeper cell onto the average cell's left edge
- below 640px a tighter phone layout takes over: short header labels, condensed chrome, and the
  player name truncates rather than forcing the table sideways. Fits 360px

## Sleeper is the spine

Sleeper's live ADP decides board membership, and it is the baseline every gap is measured
against. Three consequences worth knowing:

- **Sleeper's raw list is not just skill players.** It carries DB, FB, LB, DL and P entries with
  real ADP — one DB sits inside the top 216. Membership is filtered to QB/RB/WR/TE/K/DST. ESPN's
  slot filter used to do this for free.
- **Headshots still come from ESPN.** Sleeper's projections endpoint publishes no player id at
  all, so images are built from ESPN ids joined on the normalised name. ESPN is a comparison
  column now but remains a hard dependency for pictures. The baked snapshot stores each ESPN id
  so headshots survive an ESPN outage; a player ESPN doesn't carry gets no image and the `onerror`
  hides it. Currently that's 1 of 216.

**ESPN moved from spine to comparison, and that helped.** Its ADP is a global cross-format
aggregate, so when it was the baseline every gap on the ADP tab was measured against a number
that wasn't the league's scoring. As a comparison column it's just one mismatched source among
three, and the baseline underneath it is genuine full PPR.

ESPN's `draftRanksByRankType.PPR.rank` *is* genuinely full PPR and is no longer used, since
Sleeper has no equivalent to compare it against. The proxy still returns it.

## Failure behaviour

- any one comparison source dies → only its column falls back, per player, keyed by normalised
  name; the others stay live
- **Sleeper dies → the entire baked snapshot is kept**, because Sleeper defines who is even on
  the board
- the board renders the snapshot *before* any network call resolves, so it is never blank
- fallbacks are reported **only to the browser console** (`console.warn`), not on screen.
  The header status chips were removed and `setStatus()` is now dead code — it looks for an
  `id="srcs"` element the template no longer renders. With devtools closed there is no way
  to tell a stale column from a live one

The snapshot in `adp_data.py` is **216 rows, Sleeper-ordered, regenerated 2026-09-07** — the same
depth as the live board, so an outage no longer costs you rounds 15–18. It is one row per player
rather than the four index-aligned strings it used to be (`ROWS` / `ID_RANK` / `TOKENS` /
`ESPN_LIVE`), which had to be extended in lockstep and corrupted the fallback silently on a
single misalignment.
